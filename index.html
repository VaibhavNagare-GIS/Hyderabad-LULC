Hyderabad Land Change Atlas

Two snapshots of Hyderabad, four years apart

This page starts with a live satellite view of Hyderabad District, built from a computer model trained to recognise five kinds of ground cover: built-up land, water, forest and green cover, bare land, and floating plant growth on lakes. Below the map, you can compare how each of these categories looked in 2021 and again in 2025, check how reliable the model turned out to be, and read the explanation of what the numbers are actually showing and what is likely driving the change.

This live map shows the 2025 classification in detail. Scroll down to compare it with 2021 and read what changed and why.

About this project

How this map was built

Hyderabad District covers about 217 square kilometres and had a population of 3,943,323 at the 2011 census, according to the Telangana state government. The classified area in this study comes to roughly 191 square kilometres in both years; the remaining ground was excluded where cloud cover kept the satellite from getting a clear view.

Satellite source

Both years use Sentinel-2 Harmonized Surface Reflectance images, kept to scenes with less than 10 percent cloud cover so the classification is based on a clear view of the ground.

Classification method

A Random Forest model with 100 decision trees was trained to sort every pixel into one of five ground cover classes, using surface colour bands, short-wave infrared bands, and two vegetation and water indices as inputs.

Checking the result

Each year's map was checked against a separate set of reference points that the model never saw during training, which is how the accuracy and kappa figures below were calculated.

What each mapped class actually means on the ground, for anyone reading the map for the first time.

Built-up

Roads, roofs, and paved surfaces

Any hard, human-made surface: houses, buildings, roads, and paved open ground. This is usually the largest class in a growing city.

Waterbody

Open water

Lakes, tanks, reservoirs, and rivers where the water surface itself is visible and clear of plant cover.

Forest

Trees and dense green cover

Tree cover, parks, and other dense vegetation. It behaves differently from farmland or lawns because the canopy stays green for longer.

Barren Land

Bare, exposed ground

Land with no buildings, water, or plant cover: vacant plots, construction sites before work starts, and fallow patches of soil.

Floating Veg

Plant growth on water

Algae and floating plants such as water hyacinth sitting on the surface of a lake or tank, rather than the open water underneath.
